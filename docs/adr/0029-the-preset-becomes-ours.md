# The preset becomes ours, and it is still installed from a URL

[ADR-0023](./0023-the-preset-owns-the-chrome-the-product-owns-the-meaning.md) took
tweakcn's **Vercel** preset, and the reason was not taste: its primitives are near
monochrome — `primary: oklch(0 0 0)` — which leaves all the colour to the domain, and it
declares `font-sans: Geist`, a dependency the repository already carried.

The redesign states a ground and an accent: a midnight background at
`oklch(0.155 0.015 262)` and a mint accent at `oklch(0.82 0.14 165)`. That is not
something a domain layer says. ADR-0023 drew its line exactly there — *the domain layer
only holds what the preset cannot say* — and warned what happens without it: the layer
grows with every page and becomes the hand-written half again. A whole ground plus an
accent, carried in the domain layer, is that failure with a new name.

So the preset changes. **What does not change is how it arrives.** ADR-0023 refused
pasting — *installed from its registry rather than pasted* — and that half of the
decision is load-bearing on its own: a theme vendored as JSON stops regenerating, and
the three-block cut of `index.css` collapses into two.

The order is therefore: look in the registry for a preset that already says this, and
only if none does, publish ours on tweakcn and keep its URL.

## Consequences

- **The cut of `index.css` survives untouched.** Three blocks: the preset, never
  hand-edited and regenerated by `pnpm dlx shadcn@latest add <url>`; the domain layer,
  which does not regenerate; the `@theme inline` bridge. Only the URL changes.
- **Both allocation ramps are recomputed, and the rule that shapes them is the reason.**
  ADR-0023 runs lightness `0,45 → 0,78` on white and `0,85 → 0,52` on black because rank
  1 must be the most contrasted on each ground — *the first token in the product whose
  value depends on the ground rather than merely adapting to it*. A new ground moves both
  endpoints; the rule that rank 1 wins the contrast does not move.
- **`--loss` stays lower in chroma than `--destructive`.** The redesign raises green and
  red in lightness for the darker ground, which is the right correction for legibility
  and the wrong one to apply to chroma: ADR-0023's reason holds unchanged — the theme's
  red says *this failed*, and an unrealised loss is not an error.
- **The light theme keeps parity, because the theme is a reader's preference and not a
  design.** [ADR-0024](./0024-the-english-catalogue-is-not-a-translation.md) gives it
  three states; a ground chosen for one of them is a preset that only half exists. The
  mint accent needs its own value on white, not the dark one reused.
- **Publishing our own is a maintenance debt, and it is the cheaper one.** A theme at a
  URL we control can go stale or move; a theme pasted into the repository is guaranteed
  never to regenerate. The first failure is visible the day it happens, the second is
  invisible until the layer has grown back.
