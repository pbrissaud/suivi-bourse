# The preset owns the chrome, the product owns the meaning

The theme was never decided. `components.json` **carried** `"style": "radix-nova"` from the
prototype until #713 set it to `"new-york"`, with a long hand-written layer piled on top of it
in `index.css` and nothing saying which wins or how the preset half gets updated. It is the map's one charting blind spot: the
navigation ticket excluded it, the four page tickets assumed it, and the fog was empty.

v5 takes tweakcn's **Vercel** preset, installed from its registry rather than pasted:

```
pnpm dlx shadcn@latest add https://tweakcn.com/r/themes/vercel.json
```

The evidence for that preset over the other forty-two was already in the repository: it declares
`font-sans: Geist`, and `@fontsource-variable/geist` has been a dependency since the prototype. It
formalises an intention already present instead of replacing one. Its primitives are near
monochrome — `primary: oklch(0 0 0)` — which is what makes it right here rather than merely
pleasant: it leaves all the colour to the domain, which is
[ADR-0016](./0016-conventions-are-explained-on-the-figure.md)'s posture exactly.

The stylesheet has three blocks, and **the cut is the decision**: the preset, never hand-edited
and regenerated by the command above; the domain layer, which does not regenerate and should not;
and an `@theme inline` bridge that puts both through the same pipe, so `text-gain` is written like
`bg-card`.

## Consequences

- **The allocation is a rank ramp, not twelve hues** — decided against the session's own
  conclusion. The session held that constant lightness and chroma were required because *an
  allocation has no good or bad value*. That confused "no good value" with "no order": the slices
  are **sorted descending**, the legend follows the same order, so position pairs legend to slice
  and the legend already carries name and percentage. Colour therefore never had to identify —
  only to encode rank, redundantly with the angle, which one hue in twelve lightnesses does better
  than twelve hues. The gain is not cosmetic: twelve hues at constant lightness sit at the edge of
  discriminability and below it for a colour-blind reader.
- **Rank 1 is the most contrasted, which forces two opposite ramps.** A sequential ramp inverts
  between grounds — dark stands out on white, light stands out on black — so a single ramp would
  encode rank backwards in one theme out of two. Lightness runs 0,45 → 0,78 in light and
  0,85 → 0,52 in dark. This is the first token in the product whose value depends on the ground
  rather than merely adapting to it.
- **The domain layer only holds what the preset cannot say.** `--price` takes `--chart-2` and
  `--grant` takes `--chart-1` verbatim; only `--dividend` is added, the preset's three remaining
  slots being grey. Without that rule the layer grows with every page and becomes the hand-written
  half again.
- **`--loss` is not `--destructive`** despite two degrees of hue between them, and is deliberately
  lower in chroma. The theme's red says *this failed*; an unrealised loss is not an error, and a
  portfolio is made of small moves — a screen of vivid red reads as an alarm rather than as −0,4 %.
- **Two accepted side effects**: the radius moves from `0.625rem` to `0.5rem`, taken as the
  preset gives it, and `@fontsource-variable/geist-mono` becomes a dependency.
- **The ramp holds only because the allocation is sorted and legended.** A surface that drew those
  twelve shares without a legend, or in another order, falls outside this decision and must reopen
  it.
