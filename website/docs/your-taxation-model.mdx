---
title: Your taxation model
description: How an account is taxed, written down by you — the five shapes, where the form lives, and what the app refuses
---

A **taxation model** is how one account is taxed, written down by you. It exists
for one figure: [what you would owe if you sold
today](./read-your-figures.mdx#projected-tax), on the account's own panel.

Nothing is declared to anybody, nothing is sent anywhere, and this is not a tax
return. It is your own statement of your own wrapper, applied to today's prices.

**An account carrying no model is ordinary.** Most installs start that way, the
seeded `default` account has none, and the app shows no card at all rather than
guessing a rate — the bell carries one advisory per such account, with a link
back to it.

## Where you write it {#where}

On the **accounts** page, on the account's own panel, in the same form that
carries the account's name. There is no settings card for it and no page of its
own: a model is written where it is attached, so declaring an account and
answering the tax question are one gesture.

The field is a list, and it opens on **No model**. Below that sit the models you
have already written — **a model is reusable across accounts**, so a household
with two compte-titres writes one model and picks it twice — and last,
*Write a model…*, which opens the editor.

**Removing a model an account still carries is refused**, and the message names
the accounts. Detach it there first. Removing it from an account is a separate
gesture on that account's panel, and it leaves the model itself alone.

## The app ships no rates {#no-rates}

You type the money. Every rate on this page is one you looked up and entered,
and none of them is in the image.

That is deliberate. A rate schedule changes, differs by country, and inside one
country differs by the situation of the person reading it; a rate shipped in a
container is a tax fact that goes stale the first January nobody pulls a new
image, and it would go stale *silently*, under a figure that still looks
computed. The app would be asserting something about your taxes that it has no
way to keep true.

What does ship is **structure**, because structure is what does not move. Two
wrappers are offered as a shortcut in the editor:

| Shortcut | What it fills in |
|---|---|
| Plan d'épargne en actions (PEA) — France | a threshold at **5 years**, counted from **the first payment** |
| Assurance-vie — France | a threshold at **8 years**, counted from **the day the contract was opened** |

A shortcut fills two fields of the form and **is not stored**: pick one, the
threshold and its basis land in the editor, and from there it is your model like
any other. The rates stay empty. *None of these — I will enter it myself* is
the option for every other wrapper, and it is not a lesser path: the shortcut
list is short because only a wrapper whose structure is a stable fact belongs in
it.

**A rate is typed as a percentage.** `12,8` is what a tax schedule says, so
`12,8` is what the form takes.

## The five shapes {#shapes}

The shape is the first thing the editor asks, and it decides which fields
follow. There are five, and the list is closed.

| Shape | What it charges | What you type |
|---|---|---|
| **Not taxed** | nothing — an exempt holding, or an account you would rather the app said nothing about | nothing |
| **One rate on the gain** | the gain, when you sell, at one rate | a rate, and social charges if your wrapper has them |
| **A rate that changes with the wrapper's age** | the gain, at one rate before the wrapper reaches a certain age and another after it | both rates, the threshold in years, what the age is counted from, and social charges if any |
| **Brackets on the gain** | the gain by tranche, each bracket taxing only the part that falls inside it | the ladder, bottom to top |
| **A withholding on income** | what you receive — a dividend, a coupon — never a gain | a rate |

**Social charges are folded into the rate the card names.** Where your model
carries them, the figure and the rate shown under it both include the levy —
naming the income rate alone would contradict the figure directly above it. On
a shape that changes with age, the levy applies on **both** sides of the
threshold: a mature PEA is exempt of income tax and still owes its levy.

**The ladder's top rung has no ceiling.** *And above* is the last row and it
carries a rate only, which is what makes the ladder total: a set of bounded
brackets says nothing about a gain larger than the highest of them, and the app
would have to invent a rate for it.

**Two shapes state no figure, and they say so in words.** *Not taxed* shows
`0.00`, and that zero is the model saying so rather than something the app
computed. *A withholding on income* shows no figure at all: it is charged on
what you receive, so there is nothing about a gain for it to project.

## What the age is counted from {#age-basis}

Only the aged shape asks, and **the two answers are not the same date**. A PEA's
five years run from its first payment; an assurance-vie's eight run from the day
the contract was opened. The two coincide often enough to hide the mistake and
not often enough to make guessing safe, which is why the form asks rather than
derives.

**The day the wrapper was opened.** The form then asks for an **Opened on**
date, and that declared date is the only one used. Declare no date and there is
no figure — your first payment is not an opening, and the app will not
substitute one for the other. The field is offered pre-filled with the date of
the first payment on record; correct it if the wrapper is older than this
ledger, which is what a transfer from another broker looks like.

**The first payment into it.** The form does not ask for a date at all: the
ledger already holds one, and the earliest payment on record is what the
threshold runs from. This is what makes the PEA shortcut work end to end without
a single date being typed.

**The rate changes on the anniversary**, not the day after, and the card names
that day for as long as the threshold is still ahead. A wrapper opened on 29
February reaches its threshold on the 28th in a year that has no 29th.

## What the app refuses {#refusals}

A model is checked **when it is written**, once, so what comes back out of the
store is what went in. Each refusal below leaves the editor open with the
message, and nothing is written.

| What you wrote | Why it comes back |
|---|---|
| a required field left empty | the shape needs it; the optional one is social charges, and only on the two shapes that list it |
| a rate outside **0 – 100 %** | a rate is a fraction of something, in both directions |
| a rate and social charges **summing past 100 %** | `90 %` beside `90 %` is two ordinary-looking entries that project 180 % of a gain, and a gain cannot owe more than itself |
| a threshold that is not a whole number of years, or is negative | an age threshold is counted in years |
| a threshold **above 200 years** | no schedule surveyed goes past thirty; the bound is what keeps a typo out of date arithmetic, where an anniversary a hundred thousand years out overflows the calendar rather than producing a wrong answer |
| a ladder with **no bracket at all** | there is nothing to walk |
| a **first ceiling at or below zero** | the slice under it would be negative, and a negative slice is a *negative* tax on a real gain |
| ceilings that do not climb | the ladder is read bottom to top, and each rung taxes what falls between it and the one below |
| an upper bound on the **top** bracket, or a missing one on any other | only the top rung is open, and it is what every gain above the one below it is taxed at |
| something that is not a number where a number goes | `Infinity` and `NaN` included — Python's own JSON reader accepts both, and no tax schedule contains either |

**A stored model this version would refuse publishes no figure.** Bounds are
added over time, and a row written before one existed is read back, checked
against today's rules, and left without a projection rather than projected from
a value the app no longer accepts. Reopen the editor on it, correct it, and saving
tells you which bound it met.
