---
title: Rebuild and resolution
description: How past prices are fetched after you add events, how to follow the rebuild, and how much detail is kept as prices age
---

When you add events, SuiviBourse fetches the past prices your history needs.
This page explains how long that takes, what you see meanwhile, and how much
detail the app keeps as prices get older.

## What the rebuild does {#what}

The **History rebuild** task fetches past prices for every security you have
ever held, over the time you held it: from its first purchase to its sale, or
to today. It starts on its own within a minute of a change to your ledger.

Each run does three things for each security:

- fetches older prices, one step back toward its first purchase;
- fills the days missed while the app was stopped;
- adds the exchange rate to stored prices that lack one.

It also downloads the reference funds you follow for the
[comparison](./benchmark.mdx), one fund at a time.

## How long it takes {#duration}

With the default settings, each run makes one request per security, covering
365 days (**History depth**), and pauses 10 seconds after each request
(**Pause between requests**). Runs start every minute.

So count about one request per security and per year of history, and
10 seconds per request. For 30 securities held over 5 years, that is about
150 requests: 25 minutes or more.

You can lower **Pause between requests** in **Settings → Advanced** to go
faster. The requests then reach Yahoo Finance more often.

## Follow the progress {#progress}

- The **System** page shows a **Rebuild in progress** band, with the account
  furthest behind and the share of the period covered.
- The dashboard says how many series already go back to the first purchase,
  out of how many.
- The notifications bell takes the attention colour until the rebuild ends.
- In `/health`, the `backfill` task reads `running`, with `complete` and
  `in_scope` counts. It reads `complete` when every history is done. See
  [System](./system.mdx#health).

## What is incomplete meanwhile {#incomplete}

The app writes a day's value and return only when every security held that day
has a price. Until then:

- the portfolio value on the dashboard appears once every line has a price;
- charts start later than your first event and fill in toward the past;
- figures that need the missing days say **history still being rebuilt**;
- returns keep changing from one refresh to the next, as older days arrive.

## A security with no past prices {#no-price}

If the rebuild reaches the first purchase without finding any price, the
security is done, and the app counts the holding at its purchase price. This
usually means a mistyped symbol: see
[A symbol that stops answering](./how-prices-are-collected.mdx#failing).

If requests for a security fail 9 times in a row, the rebuild stops waiting
for it, so the rest of your history can complete. It keeps retrying in the
background. `/health` reports `backfill` as `failing`, and if the security
has no price at all, the bell shows **No price for** it.

## Resolution by age {#resolution}

The older a price, the fewer points the app keeps. Each run of the history
rebuild applies this rule to every stored price.

| Age of the price | What is kept |
|---|---|
| Under 1 year | Every point as it was stored |
| 1 to 2 years | One point per hour, the last of the hour |
| Over 2 years | One point per day, the last of the day |

The rule only removes points. It never adds any to fill a gap.

## What a rebuild cannot recover {#limits}

A rebuild fetches what Yahoo Finance still supplies:

| Period | Finest price a rebuild can fetch |
|---|---|
| The last 2 years | One per hour |
| Older | One per day |

While the app runs, it stores a price at every **Quote frequency** pass,
every 2 minutes by default. That detail exists only for the time the app was
running. A rebuild, a new installation or an import of your events cannot
bring it back.

The ledger export does not contain prices. To keep them, back up the
database file. See
[Back up and update](./backup-and-update.mdx).

## Orphan histories {#orphans}

When you delete every event of a security, its prices stay stored. The
**System** page lists them under **Orphan histories**, where **Purge** deletes
them. If you record the security again, its history is fetched again, at the
resolution the rebuild can still get.
