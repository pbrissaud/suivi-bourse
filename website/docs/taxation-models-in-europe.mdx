# Taxation models in Europe {#top}

[Your taxation model](./your-taxation-model.mdx) is typed by you, and the app
ships no rates. This page is the other half: for a common wrapper, **which
shape fits it and what its parameters were** on the day someone looked them up.

**It is a starting point, not tax advice.** Every table below is "as of" the
date beside its source, and a tax schedule changes: check each value at the
source before you enter it. The app stores what you type and nothing else — it
does not follow the law on your behalf, and it does not know your situation.

Each wrapper has the same three parts: what it is and what it holds, the
parameters to enter, and **what the model leaves out** — because a model is one
shape, and a regime is usually more than one shape can say.

## France {#fr}

### Plan d’épargne en actions (PEA) {#fr-pea}

A share-holding wrapper: listed European shares, and funds that hold them. The
tax depends on the wrapper's age, and that age runs from its **first payment**,
not from a contract date — which is why the shape asks what the age is counted
from.

<TaxationRegime id="fr/pea" />

**What the model leaves out.** A withdrawal before the threshold usually closes
the plan, which the app does not model. Social charges on a plan opened long ago
can follow older rules for the gains of past years. The option to be taxed on
the progressive scale instead is not modelled (#1106), and neither are the
special cases for unlisted securities.

### PEA-PME {#fr-pea-pme}

The companion of the PEA for small and mid-sized companies. Its tax treatment is
the PEA's, with its own payment ceiling and its own list of eligible securities.

<TaxationRegime id="fr/pea-pme" />

**What the model leaves out.** The same as the PEA. The ceiling on payments,
which is a rule about what you may put in and not about what you owe, is not a
tax parameter and the app does not check it.

### Compte-titres ordinaire (CTO) {#fr-compte-titres}

An ordinary securities account: no tax shelter, no limit on what it holds. The
gain is taxed when realised, at the flat levy, which is an income-tax part plus
social charges.

<TaxationRegime id="fr/compte-titres" />

**What the model leaves out.** The option for the progressive scale (#1106),
which can be cheaper or dearer depending on your income. Losses carried over
from earlier years, set against this year's gains. The tax on dividends, which
is a withholding and a different shape — an account carries a single model, and
this one is the gain's.

### Assurance-vie {#fr-assurance-vie}

A life-insurance contract used as a savings wrapper. The rate on a withdrawal
changes once the contract passes a certain age, counted from the day it was
**opened**. Social charges are not at the same level as on the other wrappers of
this page.

<TaxationRegime id="fr/assurance-vie" />

**What the model leaves out.** The lower rate after the threshold applies only
up to a ceiling of premiums paid, and the part above it is taxed at the higher
rate. A yearly allowance on the gains after the threshold, shared by all your
contracts. Contracts whose premiums were paid before the 2017 reform, which have
their own schedule. Succession rules, which have nothing to do with a sale.

### Plan d’épargne retraite (PER) {#fr-per}

A retirement savings plan, locked until retirement in most cases. At exit, the
tax is not on one thing: the capital that matches payments you deducted is
treated as income, and the gains are taxed at the flat levy.

<TaxationRegime id="fr/per" />

**What the model leaves out.** Almost everything about the exit of the capital
itself: the payments you deducted from your income are taxed on the progressive
scale at exit, and the model only holds the **gains'** part. If you deducted your
payments, the figure is far below what leaving the plan would cost. Early exits
for a few listed reasons, which follow their own rules.

## Germany {#de}

### Wertpapierdepot {#de-depot}

A securities account held with a bank in Germany. The bank withholds the tax on
the gain when you sell, and for most people that withholding is the end of it.

<TaxationRegime id="de/depot" />

The levy row is the solidarity surcharge, which is a percentage of the tax and
not of the gain: it is entered here as a part of the gain so that the two
figures add up.

**What the model leaves out.** The yearly allowance on investment income, which
the bank applies against your gains before it withholds. Church tax, which is due
if you belong to a church and depends on where you live. Loss offsetting between
the bank's own pots (shares and other income are not netted together). Partial
exemptions on some funds.

## Belgium {#be}

### Effectenrekening / compte-titres {#be-compte-titres}

A securities account held with a bank or a broker in Belgium. Until 2026 a
private investor managing their own savings normally owed nothing on a gain; a tax on gains on financial assets now
applies to what is realised since the start of 2026.

<TaxationRegime id="be/compte-titres" />

**What the model leaves out.** The yearly exemption on the gains, and its
carry-over. The reference value for assets bought before the tax existed, which
fixes the gain to be taxed. The tax on securities accounts, due on the account
and not on a sale. The stock-exchange tax on each trade. The withholding on
dividends and interest, which is a different shape.

## Spain {#es}

### Cuenta de valores {#es-cuenta-valores}

A securities account. The gain goes into the *base del ahorro*, taxed by
bracket: each rate applies only to the part of the gain that falls inside its
bracket, and the ceilings below are in euros.

<TaxationRegime id="es/cuenta-valores" />

The rates are the state's scale and the region's added together, as the tax
office publishes them for a region that applies the default scale.

**What the model leaves out.** Your other savings income, which fills the
brackets before the gain does: the model sees this account's gains alone, so it
starts every year from the bottom bracket. A region that sets its own scale.
Losses carried over. The rules for shares bought and sold within a short window.

## Italy {#it}

### Regime amministrato {#it-regime-amministrato}

A securities account under the *risparmio amministrato* option: the bank or
broker computes the substitute tax on each sale and pays it for you, so you
declare nothing for these gains.

<TaxationRegime id="it/regime-amministrato" />

**What the model leaves out.** The reduced rate on government bonds and
equivalent securities, which this shape cannot split from the rest. The
offsetting of losses against later gains inside the account, for a limited
number of years. The stamp duty on the account. The other tax options, which
change who computes the tax and not only how much is owed.
