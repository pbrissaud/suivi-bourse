# Taxation models in Europe {#top}

This page lists a common wrapper per country, the shape of
[taxation model](./your-taxation-model.mdx) that fits it, and what to enter.
You can [import each model](./your-taxation-model.mdx#import) in **Settings**.

Each table gives its official source and the date it was checked. Check each
value at the source before you rely on it. This page is not tax advice.

## France {#fr}

### Plan d’épargne en actions (PEA) {#fr-pea}

A share-holding wrapper: listed European shares, and funds that hold them. The
tax depends on the wrapper's age, counted from its **first payment**.

<TaxationRegime id="fr/pea" />

**What the model leaves out:**

- A withdrawal before the threshold, which usually closes the plan.
- Older social-charge rules for the past gains of a plan opened long ago.
- The option to be taxed on the progressive scale instead.
- The special cases for unlisted securities.

### PEA-PME {#fr-pea-pme}

The PEA's companion for small and mid-sized companies, taxed like the PEA.

<TaxationRegime id="fr/pea-pme" />

**What the model leaves out.** The same as the PEA. The app does not check the
ceiling on payments.

### Compte-titres ordinaire (CTO) {#fr-compte-titres}

An ordinary securities account with no tax shelter. The gain is taxed when
realised, at the flat levy: an income-tax part plus social charges.

<TaxationRegime id="fr/compte-titres" />

**What the model leaves out:**

- The option for the progressive scale, which can be cheaper or dearer
  depending on your income.
- Losses carried over from earlier years.
- The tax on dividends. It is a withholding, and an account carries a single
  model: this one is the gain's.

### Assurance-vie {#fr-assurance-vie}

A life-insurance contract used as a savings wrapper. The rate on a withdrawal
changes once the contract passes a certain age, counted from the day it was
**opened**. Social charges differ from those on the other wrappers of this
page.

<TaxationRegime id="fr/assurance-vie" />

**What the model leaves out:**

- The ceiling of premiums above which the lower rate after the threshold stops
  applying.
- The yearly allowance on gains after the threshold, shared by all your
  contracts.
- Contracts whose premiums were paid before the 2017 reform, which have their
  own schedule.
- Succession rules.

### Plan d’épargne retraite (PER) {#fr-per}

A retirement savings plan, locked until retirement in most cases. The model
applies the progressive income-tax scale and the social levies to the gains.

<TaxationRegime id="fr/per" />

**What the model leaves out:**

- The tax on the capital at exit. Payments you deducted from your income are
  taxed as income when you leave the plan, and the model counts the gains only.
- Early exits for a few listed reasons, which follow their own rules.
- The flat rate on the gains at a lump-sum exit. Taken as capital, the gains
  are taxed at 12.8 %, not on the scale, so the model is off by the gap
  between your bracket and 12.8 %.

## Germany {#de}

### Wertpapierdepot {#de-depot}

A securities account held with a bank in Germany. The bank withholds the tax on
the gain when you sell.

<TaxationRegime id="de/depot" />

The levy row is the solidarity surcharge. It is a percentage of the tax, so it
is entered here as a share of the gain that gives the same amount.

**What the model leaves out:**

- The yearly allowance on investment income, which the bank applies before it
  withholds.
- Church tax, due if you belong to a church, which depends on where you live.
- Loss offsetting between the bank's pots: shares and other income are not
  netted together.
- Partial exemptions on some funds.

## Belgium {#be}

### Effectenrekening / compte-titres {#be-compte-titres}

A securities account held with a bank or a broker in Belgium. A tax on gains on
financial assets applies to gains realised since the start of 2026.

<TaxationRegime id="be/compte-titres" />

**What the model leaves out:**

- The yearly exemption on the gains, and its carry-over.
- The reference value of assets bought before the tax existed, which fixes the
  gain to be taxed.
- The tax on securities accounts, due on the account itself.
- The stock-exchange tax on each trade.
- The withholding on dividends and interest, which is a different shape.

## Spain {#es}

### Cuenta de valores {#es-cuenta-valores}

A securities account. The gain goes into the *base del ahorro*, taxed by
bracket. The ceilings below are in euros.

<TaxationRegime id="es/cuenta-valores" />

The rates are the state's scale and the region's added together, for a region
that applies the default scale.

**What the model leaves out:**

- Your other savings income, which fills the brackets before the gain does. The
  model sees this account's gains alone, so it starts from the bottom bracket.
- A region that sets its own scale.
- Losses carried over.
- The rules for shares bought and sold within a short window.

## Italy {#it}

### Regime amministrato {#it-regime-amministrato}

A securities account under the *risparmio amministrato* option: the bank
computes and pays the substitute tax on each sale.

<TaxationRegime id="it/regime-amministrato" />

**What the model leaves out:**

- The reduced rate on government bonds and equivalent securities.
- The offsetting of losses against later gains inside the account, for a
  limited number of years.
- The stamp duty on the account.
- The other tax options, which change who computes the tax.
