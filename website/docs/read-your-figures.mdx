This page explains each figure SuiviBourse shows: what it measures and how it
is computed. The examples use invented amounts in euros.

## Average purchase price {#avg-cost}

A line's cost is tracked by weighted average. A purchase adds its quantity and
its price plus fee to the line. A sale removes a share of the cost in
proportion to the quantity sold, so the average does not move.

```
average purchase price = cost of the shares held / quantity held
```

| Event | Quantity | Cost | Average |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy 10 at 50, fee 5 | 10 | 505.00 | 50.50 |
| Buy 10 at 60 | 20 | 1 105.00 | 55.25 |
| Sell 5 at 70, fee 2 | 15 | 828.75 | 55.25 |

A free share grant adds its quantity at the price you declared, or at zero if
you left the price blank. There is no FIFO option.

## Total P&L {#total-gain}

Your total gain is the sum of four terms:

```
Total P&L = unrealised + realised + dividends − transfer fees
```

It includes lines you have sold. The percentage beside it is the total P&L
divided by your [net contributed](#net-contributed). An account's **Gain** is
the same sum for that account, and **Where the gain comes from** on the account
page lists its four terms.

### Unrealised P&L {#latent-gain}

The market value of the shares you hold, minus their cost.

```
unrealised = quantity held × price − cost of the shares held
```

In the example above, 15 shares at 62 are worth 930.00. Their cost is 828.75,
so the unrealised gain is 101.25. The **Gain** column and the **Securities
gain** line of the **Shares** page show this term.

### Realised P&L {#realized-gain}

What a sale brought in above the cost it removed.

```
realised = quantity sold × price − fee − quantity sold × average purchase price
```

In the example, the sale of 5 at 70 brings 348.00 after the fee and removes
276.25 of cost, so the realised gain is 71.75. The money is already in your
cash, so it is not added to your portfolio value a second time.

### Dividends {#dividends}

The dividends you received, net of the fee recorded with each one. A dividend
does not lower the cost of the line and does not change its unrealised gain.

### Transfer fees {#deposit-fees}

The fees recorded on your deposits and withdrawals. They belong to no line, so
they are a term of their own. The account page shows **Transfer fees** only
when they are not zero.

## Net contributed {#net-contributed}

The money you put in, less the money you took out.

```
net contributed = deposits − withdrawals + free shares at their declared value
```

Deposits and withdrawals count at their amount before any fee. Example: you
deposit 1 000 with a fee of 2, then 500, then withdraw 200. Net contributed is
1 300, and the fee of 2 appears in [transfer fees](#deposit-fees). When free
shares count, the dashboard says **including … of free shares**.

Prices never move this figure.

## Portfolio value and its change {#value}

**Portfolio value** is your cash plus the market value of your securities. If
you record no deposit or withdrawal, the dashboard shows **Securities** instead:
the value of your lines alone.

The line under it gives the change over the chart's period (**1 month**,
**This year**, **1 year**, **All**):

- the amount is the change in your total P&L over the period, so a deposit is
  not counted as a gain. The line says **excluding deposits** when money came in
  or went out during the period;
- the percentage is that amount divided by the portfolio value on the first day
  of the period;
- for **All**, the amount is your whole total P&L and the percentage is over
  your net contributed.

## Performance {#twr}

The **Performance** view of the dashboard chart shows a time-weighted return:
how the money in the portfolio grew, with deposits and withdrawals taken out.

The series starts at 100 on the first day the portfolio has a value. Each day:

```
index today = index yesterday × (value today − money in today) / value yesterday
```

"Money in" is deposits minus withdrawals, plus free shares at their declared
value. The value includes your cash. The chart shows the change from the first
day of the period you pick.

Example: the portfolio is worth 1 000. The next day you deposit 500 and it is
worth 1 530. The index moves by (1 530 − 500) / 1 000, that is +3 %.

While history is still being rebuilt, the start of the series moves back and the
curve can change without any price changing. See
[Rebuild and resolution](./rebuild-and-resolution.mdx).

It can differ from the [money-weighted return](#xirr), even in sign, because it
ignores when your money arrived.

## Money-weighted return {#xirr}

The annual rate your money earned, taking into account when each amount went in
or out. The app finds the rate *r* that makes this sum zero:

```
Σ flow / (1 + r) ^ (days since the first flow / 365)
```

Deposits and free shares count as negative flows, withdrawals as positive flows,
and today's value as a final positive flow.

Example: you deposit 1 000 on 1 January and 1 000 on 1 July. On the next
1 January the account is worth 2 100. The return is about 6.7 % a year, because
the second deposit worked for half a year only.

The dashboard shows it as **Money-weighted return … a year**, an account as
**MWR**. It is absent when the account has no deposit, no withdrawal and no free
share grant, while history is being rebuilt, or when every flow falls on the
same day.

## Estimated tax {#projected-tax}

An account with a [taxation model](./your-taxation-model.mdx) shows **If I sell
today**: what you would owe if you sold everything in it today.

The card lists the **Taxable gain**, the **Income tax**, the **Social levies**,
their sum as **Estimated tax**, and the **Rate applied**, levies included. The
large figure, **net after tax**, is the account value minus the estimated tax.

The base depends on the shape of the model:

| Shape | Taxable gain | Tax |
|---|---|---|
| **One rate on the gain** | The account's [unrealised gain](#latent-gain) | base × (rate + social charges) |
| **A rate that changes with the wrapper's age** | Account value − [net contributed](#net-contributed) | base × (rate in force today + social charges) |
| **Brackets on the gain** | The account's unrealised gain | each bracket taxes the part of the base inside it, plus social charges on all of it |

Example with brackets: up to 6 000 at 19 %, up to 50 000 at 21 %, and above at
23 %. A taxable gain of 10 000 owes 6 000 × 19 % + 4 000 × 21 % = 1 980.

A base below zero counts as zero. Each account is taken on its own, so a loss in
one does not reduce the tax of another. **Not taxed** and **A withholding on
income** show no tax. If one held line has no value, the account shows no
estimate.

For the age shape, see
[What the age is counted from](./your-taxation-model.mdx#age-basis). The card
says **The rate changes on …** while that day is ahead.

An account without a model shows **no taxation model** and a **Choose a model**
button. On the dashboard, **After tax if all sold** is the portfolio value minus
the estimated tax of the accounts that have one. With no model anywhere, the
line reads **If all sold, before tax**.

## A line with no price {#carrying-price}

Some securities never get a price. Once the app has stopped looking, it values
such a line at its own cost: its average purchase price. Its unrealised gain is
then zero.

While the app is still rebuilding history for that security, the line says
**history still being rebuilt** instead. A security quoted without a currency is
also valued at its cost. The dashboard lists these lines under **No price:**.

A security with a price but no exchange rate into your base currency is a
different case: it shows its own price and says **waiting for a rate**.

## When a figure is missing {#absence}

An em dash (—) means there is nothing to compute. A figure that is missing for a
reason says so in words.

| Case | Price | Value | Unrealised |
|---|---|---|---|
| No price, valued at cost | — | its cost | 0.00 |
| Price known, no exchange rate | its own price | waiting for a rate | waiting for a rate |
| Line sold entirely | — | 0.00 | — |
| History being rebuilt | history still being rebuilt | history still being rebuilt | history still being rebuilt |
| Asked several times, no price | a count, for example 17 consecutive readings, no price | its cost | 0.00 |

The last case usually comes from a wrong symbol: check it in the ledger.

## Investment rhythm {#rhythm}

**Investment rhythm** on the dashboard looks at your purchases over the last
twelve months. **bought a month (median)** is the median of the monthly totals
of your purchases, counting only the months with at least one purchase. The bars
show each month, and the caption names the months with no purchase.

## Comparison with a fund {#benchmark}

The **Comparison** page answers one question in euros: would an index fund have
left you with more, for the same money, on the same days?

The app replays your money into the fund:

- each purchase buys fund shares for the same amount, on the same day;
- each sale and each dividend sells fund shares for the same amount;
- a free share grant buys fund shares at its declared value, so a grant with no
  price gives the fund nothing and counts in your favour;
- a deposit that stays in cash buys nothing.

**Same money invested** is the sum of those flows. **Gap** is the value of your
securities minus the value of the fund shares. **Gain on your securities** and
**Gain on the …** are each value minus the same money invested, divided by the
same money invested. They cover the period and are not annual rates.

Example: you buy 1 000 in March and 1 000 in September. Today your securities
are worth 2 300. The same flows into the fund would be worth 2 200. The gap is
+100, your gain is +15 % and the fund's is +10 %.

The period starts at your first purchase, or at the fund's first quote if that
is later. [Comparison](./benchmark.mdx#period) lists the cases that shorten it.
